[Image1] is the house above ground. [Image2] is the same house below ground in
the Upside Down. [Image3] is the plot above ground. [Image4] is the same plot
below ground. Take all appearance from these four images. Never blend the two
worlds' looks.

SCENE. Hawkins, Indiana, 1983. A large two-storey suburban family home, and the
identical house mirrored directly beneath it underground in the Upside Down. One
unbroken take travelling from one world into the other.

ABOVE (per Image1, Image3): brick and cream clapboard house, covered porch, bay
window, dormers, attached garage, driveway, sun-bleached lawn, stone path, kerb,
pines behind. Clear hot afternoon, hard warm sun, deep blue sky, long shadows.

BELOW (per Image2, Image4): the same house and plot, mirrored, drained to ashen
grey. Wrapped in wet tendrils and vine, windows dead but for a dull red seep.
Ground under organic membrane, pines black skeletons, spores drifting like snow,
churning black and blood-red storm sky with silent lightning. Nothing alive.

CAMERA. One continuous Steadicam take, no cuts, always aimed at the house.

THE RULE: from the moment the camera begins to climb until it finally settles,
its travel through space and its rotation happen together as one inseparable
motion. The picture turns steadily the whole time the camera is moving. The
rotation never pauses and never happens as a separate move after arriving. One
smooth half-turn spread evenly across the whole journey.

BEAT 1 - PULL BACK. Open at eye level two metres up, filling frame with the
front door and porch. Pull straight back at that height until the whole house,
lawn and driveway are in frame, ground line across the middle. Horizon dead
level, no rotation yet. Longest beat.

BEAT 2 - RISE AND TURN AS ONE. Without stopping, the camera starts climbing and
starts turning in the same instant, arcing up and over the house, tilting down,
until directly above the roof looking straight down - by which point the picture
has turned a quarter over and the horizon runs vertically.

BEAT 3 - THROUGH THE GROUND. Still turning at the same rate, it carries over the
ridge, drops fast down the far side behind the house, and passes straight
through the ground plane. At the instant it breaks through, the picture is
already well past a quarter turn. It must not be level here.

BEAT 4 - BENEATH. Below ground now, descending until far under the mirrored
house looking up at its underside, then swinging back up toward the front. The
rotation crosses past halfway.

BEAT 5 - ARRIVE ALREADY RIGHT WAY UP. Rise to face the front of the underground
house as the rotation completes a full half turn at the moment of arrival. The
camera is fully inverted, so the underground house reads upright and level - the
mirror of the opening frame. Settle and hold.

DO NOT hold the camera level through the descent and then rotate at the end. Do
not arrive with the house upside down and then spin it upright. By the time the
underground house is in frame the turn is already finished.

STYLE. Photoreal, anamorphic, 35mm film grain, Stranger Things cinematography.
Single continuous take, smooth constant motion, no cuts, no speed ramps, no
shake. No people, animals, vehicles or text.
