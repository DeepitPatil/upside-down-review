A large two-storey American suburban family home, Midwest, 1983. Wide,
sprawling, clearly a family house rather than a cottage.

Structure: a cross-gable roof in weathered red-brown asphalt shingle, with two
gabled dormers set into the front slope. The ground floor is clad in red-brown
brick veneer; the upper storey in cream-white painted horizontal clapboard
siding, with a white trim band dividing them.

Front: a deep covered porch running across the left half of the facade, roofed,
carried on four square white posts with a white railing between them. A dark
green panelled front door with a small rectangular window and brass hardware,
set under the porch, with a black wall lantern beside it. To the right of the
porch, a large ground-floor bay window projecting forward, three-sided, with
white frames. Four evenly spaced double-hung windows across the upper storey,
white frames, flanked by dark green louvred shutters.

Garage: an attached two-car garage on the right, projecting forward of the main
facade, with its own gable above. A white segmented up-and-over garage door with
a row of four small square windows across its top panel. A short apron of grey
concrete driveway directly in front of it.

Details: a red brick chimney rising on the left gable end. White-painted eaves
and fascia. Aluminium gutters and one downspout at each corner. Weathered but
maintained — this is a lived-in family home, not a showhome.
