[Image1] is the house above ground. [Image2] is the same house below ground in
the Upside Down. [Image3] is the plot above ground. [Image4] is the same plot
below ground. Take all appearance from these four images. Keep the two worlds
visually separate and never blend their looks.

SCENE. Hawkins, Indiana, summer 1983. A large two-storey suburban family home
alone on a quiet residential street, and the identical house mirrored directly
beneath it underground, in the Upside Down. One unbroken handheld-smooth
Steadicam take, no cuts, that travels from one world into the other.

ABOVE GROUND, per [Image1] and [Image3]. Brick ground floor, cream clapboard
upper storey, red-brown shingled cross-gable roof with two dormers, deep covered
front porch on white posts, projecting bay window, dark green shutters and a
dark green front door, attached garage on the right with a concrete driveway.
Wide sun-bleached lawn, stone slab path, kerb and cracked asphalt street,
mailbox on a weathered post, utility pole and cables, dense pines behind. Clear
hot afternoon, hard warm sunlight from the front left, deep blue sky, long soft
shadows.

BELOW GROUND, per [Image2] and [Image4]. The same house on the same plot,
mirrored, everything drained to ashen grey and cold blue-black. House wrapped in
slick rope-like tendrils and creeping vine, windows dead but for a dull
ember-red seep. Ground swallowed under wet organic membrane. Pines reduced to
black leafless skeletons. Spores suspended at every depth, drifting like snow.
Churning black and blood-red storm sky lit from within by silent forked
lightning. No sun, no wind, nothing alive.

CAMERA, second by second. The camera is always aimed at the centre of the house
and holds a constant distance of roughly 23 metres once the pull-back ends.

0-1s   Eye level, 2 m off the ground, 11 m from the porch, looking slightly up
       at the front door. Begin pulling straight back. Level horizon.
1-2s   Continue pulling back to 20 m. Height stays at 2 m. The full house and
       front lawn come into frame. Horizon still level.
2-3s   Settle at 23 m and hold for a beat. The whole property is in frame, the
       ground line across the middle of the shot.
3-4s   Begin to rise and roll together. Climb to 12 m, tilting down to about 30
       degrees above the house. The frame rolls 30 degrees clockwise.
4-5s   Continue up and over. Climb to 24 m, almost directly above the roof
       looking straight down. The frame has rolled 90 degrees - the horizon now
       runs vertically down the picture.
5-6s   Pass over the ridge and start down the far side, behind the house.
       Descend hard through 15 m, then through zero, punching through the
       ground plane itself at about 5.8s. The frame is rolled past 90 degrees
       and still turning.
6-7s   Now below ground in the Upside Down. Keep descending to the lowest point,
       24 m beneath the house, looking up at its underside. The mirrored house
       fills the frame from below.
7-8s   Begin rising back toward the front of the house, still underground,
       climbing from 24 m below to 9 m below. The frame rolls through 130 to
       155 degrees - the world is nearly inverted.
8-9s   Rise to 3 m below ground and level out facing the front of the
       underground house. The roll completes at 175 degrees: the shot is now
       fully upside down relative to where it began, which makes the
       underground house read upright and perfectly framed.
9-10s  Hold. Camera at rest 2 m below the ground plane, 23 m from the house,
       looking straight at the front of the Upside Down house. Final frame is
       the exact mirror of the opening composition.

STYLE. Photoreal, anamorphic, shallow depth of field, 35 mm film grain, Netflix
Stranger Things cinematography. Continuous single take, smooth constant motion,
no cuts, no speed ramps, no camera shake. No people, no animals, no vehicles,
no text.
