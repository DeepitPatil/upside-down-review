[Image1] is the house above ground. [Image2] is the same house below ground in
the Upside Down. [Image3] is the plot above ground. [Image4] is the same plot
below ground. Take all appearance from these four images. Keep the two worlds
visually separate and never blend their looks.

SCENE. Hawkins, Indiana, summer 1983. A large two-storey suburban family home
alone on a quiet residential street, and the identical house mirrored directly
beneath it underground, in the Upside Down. One unbroken handheld-smooth
Steadicam take, no cuts, that travels from one world into the other.

ABOVE GROUND, per [Image1] and [Image3]. Brick ground floor, cream clapboard
upper storey, red-brown shingled cross-gable roof with two dormers, deep covered
front porch on white posts, projecting bay window, dark green shutters and a
dark green front door, attached garage on the right with a concrete driveway.
Wide sun-bleached lawn, stone slab path, kerb and cracked asphalt street,
mailbox on a weathered post, utility pole and cables, dense pines behind. Clear
hot afternoon, hard warm sunlight from the front left, deep blue sky, long soft
shadows.

BELOW GROUND, per [Image2] and [Image4]. The same house on the same plot,
mirrored, everything drained to ashen grey and cold blue-black. House wrapped in
slick rope-like tendrils and creeping vine, windows dead but for a dull
ember-red seep. Ground swallowed under wet organic membrane. Pines reduced to
black leafless skeletons. Spores suspended at every depth, drifting like snow.
Churning black and blood-red storm sky lit from within by silent forked
lightning. No sun, no wind, nothing alive.

CAMERA. One unbroken Steadicam take, no cuts. The camera is always aimed at the
centre of the house and holds a constant distance of roughly 23 metres once the
pull-back ends.

THE ONE RULE THAT GOVERNS THIS SHOT. From the moment the camera begins to climb
until the moment it finally settles, its movement through space and its rotation
happen together, continuously, as one single inseparable motion. The picture
turns steadily the entire time the camera is travelling. The rotation never
pauses, never races ahead, and never happens as a separate move once the camera
has already arrived somewhere. Spread one smooth half-turn evenly across the
whole journey.

BEAT 1 - PULL BACK. Open at eye level, two metres off the ground, close enough
to fill the frame with the front door and porch. Pull straight back, holding
that same low height, until the whole house, lawn and driveway sit in frame with
the ground line across the middle. Horizon dead level. No rotation at all yet.
Settle for a beat. This beat is the longest quarter of the shot.

BEAT 2 - RISE AND TURN AS ONE. Without stopping, the camera starts to climb and
starts to turn in the same instant. It arcs up and forward over the house,
tilting down as it goes, until it is directly above the roof looking straight
down. By the time it reaches the top of that arc the picture has turned a
quarter of the way over, so the horizon now runs vertically down the frame.

BEAT 3 - THROUGH THE GROUND. Still turning at the same steady rate, the camera
carries over the ridge and drops down the far side behind the house, descending
fast, and passes straight down through the ground plane itself. At the instant
it breaks through, the picture is already well past a quarter turn and still
going. It must not be level here.

BEAT 4 - BENEATH THE UPSIDE DOWN. Now below ground. The camera keeps descending
until it is far under the mirrored house, looking up at its underside, then
begins swinging back up toward the front. The rotation crosses past halfway
during this beat.

BEAT 5 - ARRIVE ALREADY RIGHT WAY UP. The camera rises to face the front of the
underground house, and the rotation completes a full half turn at exactly the
moment it arrives. Because the camera is now fully inverted, the underground
house reads upright, level and perfectly framed - the mirror of the opening
composition. Settle and hold, motionless, for the last moment of the shot.

WHAT NOT TO DO. Do not hold the camera level through the descent and then rotate
the picture at the end. Do not arrive in the lower world with the house upside
down and then spin it upright. By the time the underground house is in frame the
turn must already be finished. The rotation belongs to the journey, not to the
arrival.

STYLE. Photoreal, anamorphic, shallow depth of field, 35 mm film grain, Netflix
Stranger Things cinematography. Continuous single take, smooth constant motion,
no cuts, no speed ramps, no camera shake. No people, no animals, no vehicles,
no text.
